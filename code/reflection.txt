This assignment was about taking twelve messy payroll CSVs and turning them into one clean history and a Streamlit report. The main thing I learned was grain, which is what one row represents. The history is one row per person per week, and summarize_employees uses a group by with named aggregation to make a new table that's one row per person. That's fine under the lineage rule as long as the original history doesn't get changed. Cleaning the data was harder than I thought it would be. IDs like "E4" didn't match the roster after .str.upper(), so I had to use .str.zfill(3) to make them "E004". The dates were in two formats, so I used pd.to_datetime twice with a format= each time, and anything bad turned into NaT. I also learned ignore_index=True in pd.concat stops you from getting twelve row 0s. The part that made the most sense to me was missed_weeks. Nobody's absence is an actual row in the data, but pivot_table with fill_value=0 creates it, and then melt turns it back into rows I can filter. I got stuck when test_code.py said grid.columns.name = None broke the lineage rule, and I fixed it with .rename_axis(columns=None). Next I want to get more comfortable writing groupby, pivot_table and melt from scratch, especially picking the right index, columns and aggfunc, and I want to look more into rename_axis since I didn't know a pivot's columns had a name at all.
